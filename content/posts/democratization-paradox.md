Lowering barriers to entry in any field reliably triggers the same pattern: output explodes, average quality collapses, and the system's ability to surface genuine innovation degrades. This is not a bug of any single technology or industry — it is a structural phenomenon observable across software, science, music, finance, photography, journalism, and virtually every domain where tools or platforms have made participation dramatically easier. The tension between access and quality is as old as the printing press, but AI and digital platforms have accelerated it to a degree that overwhelms traditional filtering mechanisms.

The critical insight from the research is that democratization itself is neither good nor bad — outcomes depend almost entirely on whether effective curation and feedback mechanisms exist to separate signal from noise. When they do, a larger pool of participants generates more genuine breakthroughs. When they don't, noise buries signal and degrades the entire field.

## Sturgeon's Law meets the attention economy

No single academic term captures this phenomenon perfectly, but several overlapping frameworks describe its mechanics. Theodore Sturgeon's 1958 observation that "ninety percent of everything is crap" was originally a defense of science fiction [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon's_law) — the genre wasn't uniquely bad, it was just subject to the same ratio as everything else. [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Sturgeon's_law) The deeper implication, now playing out at unprecedented scale, is that when barriers fall and volume increases a hundredfold, the absolute quantity of low-quality work grows from a manageable nuisance to a drowning flood. The ratio stays constant, but the experience of navigating the field transforms completely. [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/sturgeons-law/)

Herbert Simon identified the bottleneck in 1971: "A wealth of information creates a poverty of attention." [Oxford Reference](https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/acref/9780191843730.001.0001/q-oro-ed5-00019845) [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author/quotes/89879.Herbert_A_Simon) Human attention is fixed while information supply grows exponentially. Simon recognized that system designers consistently misframe the problem as information scarcity when the real challenge is attention allocation — building filters, not firehoses. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_economy) This insight underpins the entire attention economy literature, from Tim Wu's *The Attention Merchants* to Barry Schwartz's paradox of choice research showing that consumers confronted with overwhelming options become passive, default to familiar brands, and paradoxically make worse decisions. [Medium](https://medium.com/@kylebylin/paradox-or-paradise-91d203e4248b)

Andrew Keen's *The Cult of the Amateur* (2007) mounted the most direct critique, arguing that Web 2.0's promise of democratized media [Bookey](https://www.bookey.app/book/the-cult-of-the-amateur) delivered "an endless digital forest of mediocrity" — superficial observation replacing deep analysis. Jaron Lanier, a technologist critiquing from inside, warned that digital collectivism "undervalues humans in favor of anonymity and crowd identity" and that when everyone collaborates on everything, "you generate a dull, average outcome." [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/6683549-you-are-not-a-gadget) [Institute of Network Cultures](https://networkcultures.org/cpov/resources/resources_in_english/response-to-jaron-lanier-digital-maoism/) Tom Nichols extended this to expertise itself, arguing in *The Death of Expertise* that we have reached "a Google-fueled, Wikipedia-based, blog-sodden collapse of any division between professionals and laypeople." [Radenintan](https://dmpi.pasca.radenintan.ac.id/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2018/11/The-Death-of-Expertise.pdf) A 2024 Pew survey found only 47% of Americans believe scientists should play a major role in policy decisions. [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/The_Death_of_Expertise)

Clayton Christensen's disruptive innovation theory provides a crucial counterweight: low-quality newcomers entering at the bottom of a market is exactly how disruption works. [Christenseninstitute +2](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/disruptive-innovation/) The analytical challenge Christensen identified is distinguishing genuine disruptors — who start crude but improve relentlessly — from copycat commoditizers who simply add noise. [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2015/12/what-is-disruptive-innovation) Eric von Hippel's MIT research further complicates the picture by demonstrating that in some industries, over 80% of functionally new innovations come from users, not manufacturers, because users possess "sticky" local knowledge about their own needs. [SSRN +3](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=712763) But von Hippel's framework implicitly supports the quality concern: innovation concentrates among a small subset of sophisticated "lead users," [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23573725_Democratizing_Innovation) not the mass of newcomers. [Internet Archive +2](https://archive.org/details/mit_press_book_9780262285636)

## The evidence spans every field imaginable

The pattern repeats with striking consistency across domains that share nothing in common except lowered barriers.

In software development, the phenomenon has a name: "vibe coding," coined by Andrej Karpathy in February 2025 to describe "fully giving in to the vibes" while using AI to generate code without understanding it. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_coding) The data is stark. GitClear's analysis of 211 million lines of code found that code refactoring dropped from 25% of changed lines in 2021 to under 10% by 2024, while copy-pasted code surged 48%. [GitClear](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research) Code churn — lines rewritten within two weeks of being authored — rose from 3.1% to 5.7%, indicating code that wasn't understood when written. [Jonas](https://www.jonas.rs/2025/02/09/report-summary-gitclear-ai-code-quality-research-2025.html) Veracode found 45% of AI-generated code contains known security flaws, with that rate "completely flat" over two years despite tool improvements. [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/application-security/ai-generated-code-leading-expanded-technical-security-debt) Apiiro's analysis of Fortune 50 enterprises showed a tenfold increase in security findings per month between December 2024 and June 2025. [Pixelmojo](https://www.pixelmojo.io/blogs/vibe-coding-technical-debt-crisis-2026-2027) One veteran API engineer summarized it: "I don't think I have ever seen so much technical debt being created in such a short period of time during my 35-year career in technology." [LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/technical-direction/how-ai-generated-code-accelerates-technical-debt)

Open source software faces a related crisis. GitHub, hosting 300 million repositories and 100 million developers, [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/tutorials/most-popular-github-repos) is now contending with what its own product manager called "a critical issue" — AI-generated contributions overwhelming maintainers. [Open Source For You](https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/02/github-weighs-pull-request-kill-switch-as-ai-slop-floods-open-source/) [GitHub](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/185387) Daniel Stenberg, who maintains curl (one of the internet's most critical tools), found AI-generated reports grew to 20% of bug bounty submissions, [Andrew Nesbitt](https://nesbitt.io/2026/02/13/respectful-open-source.html) none identifying actual vulnerabilities. He killed the bug bounty program entirely. [IT Pro](https://www.itpro.com/software/open-source/curl-open-source-bug-bounty-program-scrapped) A University of Wisconsin study found that only 0.5% of GitHub repositories include all essential quality elements. [Wisc](https://ospo.wisc.edu/blog/2024/repo-quality-metrics/) The npm ecosystem, with 3.1 million packages and no vetting process, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Npm) has produced cascading failures — the infamous left-pad incident in 2016, where deleting an 11-line package broke builds at Facebook, Netflix, and Spotify, exposed how deeply fragile the dependency ecosystem had become. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Npm_left-pad_incident) [HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Npm_left-pad_incident)

Scientific publishing exhibits the same dynamics at slower timescales. Publication volume grows at 5–9% annually, [Ouvrir la Science](https://www.ouvrirlascience.fr/excessive-growth-in-the-number-of-scientific-publications/) with over 5 million academic articles published per year. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844023035235) [WordsRated](https://wordsrated.com/number-of-academic-papers-published-per-year/) Predatory journals — which accept papers with minimal or no review — expanded from 18 publishers on Beall's List in 2011 to 923 by 2016, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beall's_List) encompassing an estimated 21,735 journals. [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10328228/) In John Bohannon's 2013 sting for *Science*, 82% of predatory publishers accepted a fabricated paper. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beall's_List) Meanwhile, a landmark 2016 Nature survey of 1,500 researchers found over 70% could not reproduce another scientist's experiment. [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/biopharma/news/scientists-blame-publish-or-perish-culture-for-reproducibility-crisis-395293) Peter Higgs, whose theoretical work led to the discovery of the Higgs boson, said in 2013 that he "wouldn't get an academic job today" because he wouldn't be considered productive enough. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publish_or_perish) The publish-or-perish incentive structure rewards volume over rigor, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214804324001642) and irreproducible research wastes an estimated $28 billion annually in the US alone.

The creative arts offer perhaps the most visceral illustrations. Spotify receives roughly 100,000 new tracks daily [Owebest](https://www.owebest.com/blog/spotify-statistics) — more music in a single day than was released in the entire year of 1989. [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/more-music-released-single-day-2024-than-entirety-1989-study-streaming-news) Yet 175.5 million of its 202 million tracks fail to reach the 1,000-play threshold required for royalty payments. [Chaoszine](https://chaoszine.net/according-to-a-new-report-99-000-songs-are-being-uploaded-to-streaming-services-every-day-most-of-them-go-unheard/) Ninety-five percent of artists have fewer than 1,000 monthly listeners. [Soundcamps](https://soundcamps.com/blog/spotify-statistics/) [Chaoszine](https://chaoszine.net/according-to-a-new-report-99-000-songs-are-being-uploaded-to-streaming-services-every-day-most-of-them-go-unheard/) Photography tells a similar story: 2.1 trillion photos are taken annually, smartphones capture 94% of them, [Photo Tutorial](https://photutorial.com/photos-statistics/) and global camera shipments have fallen 94% since 2010. [Electro IQ](https://electroiq.com/stats/mobile-photography-statistics/) Amazon's Kindle Direct Publishing has been flooded with AI-generated books to the point where Amazon capped self-publishing at three titles per day per account. [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/discussion/are-ai-generated-books-problem-amazon/) The science fiction magazine Clarkesworld had to temporarily close submissions entirely after an overwhelming wave of AI-generated stories — its editor warned the ironic result would be higher barriers for legitimate new authors. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_slop)

In finance, zero-commission trading apps created what amounts to a natural experiment in barrier-lowering. Robinhood's 25 million funded accounts [Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/robinhood-statistics/) include 50% first-time investors who hold an average of just three stocks. [Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/how-robinhood-plays-your-attention-and-makes-you-lose-money) Academic research found that 70–75% of retail traders lose money, and retail investors lost an estimated $1.14 billion trading options alone between 2019 and 2021, plus another $4.13 billion in hidden execution costs. [OnlyFunds](https://www.onlyfunds.com/news-and-insights/everyday-traders-on-meme-stocks-lost-big) The SPAC boom magnified these dynamics on the corporate side: 613 SPACs raised $162 billion in 2021, [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/research/spac-statistics-ipos/) but de-SPAC companies subsequently lost an average of 67% of their value, [Foley & Lardner LLP](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/09/spac-4-0-from-spectacular-failures-to-a-disciplined-renaissance/) with Bloomberg estimating $46 billion in losses from SPACs that went bankrupt in 2023. [Better Markets](https://bettermarkets.org/analysis/spacs-crypto-buyer-beware/)

## The hidden costs fall on everyone except the noise-makers

The flood's costs are distributed unevenly and often invisibly. The most pernicious effect is cost-shifting: when barriers to entry fall, the cost of quality assessment doesn't disappear — it transfers from producers (who previously had to invest in overcoming barriers, demonstrating commitment) to consumers, reviewers, and maintainers who must now sort through vastly more material with no more time or attention than before.

In academic peer review, average review times now exceed 100 days, with editors routinely contacting 8–12 potential reviewers before finding two willing to serve. [Editors Cafe](https://editorscafe.org/details.php?id=68) NeurIPS acknowledged that its reviewer recruitment campaigns now draw more junior, less experienced reviewers. The 2026 ICML conference received over 24,000 submissions, more than double the prior year's count. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-03967-9) The publishing industry generates approximately 40% profit margins while researchers write, referee, and edit for free [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11804702/) [Sciencecast](https://sciencecast.org/blog/articles) — a system now straining past its capacity.

Decision fatigue compounds the problem at the consumer level. Iyengar and Lepper's foundational research demonstrated that [Acr-journal](https://acr-journal.com/article/cognitive-biases-in-digital-decision-making-how-consumers-navigate-information-overload-consumer-behavior--889/) people presented with fewer options are more likely to choose and follow through. [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8567038/) ERP neurological studies confirm that under information overload, consumers literally invest fewer attentional resources in processing — the brain disengages. [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8567038/) When fatigued, consumers default to brands they already know, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_fatigue) which paradoxically advantages incumbents and hurts the newcomers that democratization was supposed to empower.

The concept of "AI slop" — selected as 2025 Word of the Year by Merriam-Webster [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_slop) — captures the quality-perception damage. Scholars have identified three defining properties of slop: superficial competence, asymmetric effort, and mass producibility. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_slop) It looks adequate on the surface but was produced with minimal investment and can be generated at enormous scale. This creates a feedback loop: algorithms optimize for engagement rather than quality, slop generates engagement, platforms surface more slop, and the field's perceived quality degrades for everyone — including those producing genuine work. [MoinAI](https://www.moin.ai/en/chatbot-wiki/ai-slop)

Crypto fraud illustrates the reputational cost at its extreme: $35 billion flowed to cryptocurrency fraud schemes in 2025, [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/reports-and-whitepapers/2026-crypto-crime-report) with illicit crypto volume reaching $158 billion, [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/reports-and-whitepapers/2026-crypto-crime-report) eroding trust in legitimate financial technology.

The devaluation of expertise may be the deepest long-term cost. [MoinAI](https://www.moin.ai/en/chatbot-wiki/ai-slop) When AI tools enable anyone to produce work that superficially resembles expert output, the perceived gap between amateurs and professionals narrows — even when the actual gap remains enormous. The workforce pipeline paradox in software engineering captures this perfectly: 54% of engineering leaders plan to hire fewer junior developers because AI handles basic tasks, but AI-generated technical debt specifically requires the human judgment that juniors develop through years of hands-on experience. [Pixelmojo](https://www.pixelmojo.io/blogs/vibe-coding-technical-debt-crisis-2026-2027) The field is simultaneously eliminating the training ground for expertise while generating unprecedented demand for it.

## The case for democratization remains powerful despite the noise

The critique of lowered barriers, however compelling, must contend with an overwhelming historical record of outsiders producing transformative innovations precisely because barriers fell. The personal computer revolution happened because hobbyists and dropouts — Jobs, Wozniak, Gates — built machines that IBM, DEC, and every major computing company had explicitly refused to create. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/technology/computer/The-personal-computer-revolution) Mainframe incumbents weren't protecting quality; they were protecting margins. [Medium](https://medium.com/@nareshnavinash/personal-computing-industry-demand-side-disruption-ddbbebbb0395)

Linux, created by a 21-year-old Finnish student, now runs the majority of the world's servers, all Android devices, and most supercomputers. Wikipedia, built entirely by volunteers, became the most comprehensive encyclopedia in history. [Joey Cofone](https://www.joeycofone.com/eureka-newsletter/sturgeons-law)

The gatekeeping critique cuts especially deep because of documented systemic biases. J.K. Rowling's Harry Potter was rejected by 12 publishers [Hptbydts](https://blog.hptbydts.com/rejected-by-12-publishers.-the-most-successful-book-series-in-history) before a chairman's 8-year-old daughter demanded to read the rest; [LitReactor](https://litreactor.com/columns/5-famous-bestsellers-that-were-rejected-and-50-more) the series has sold 450 million copies. [LitReactor](https://litreactor.com/columns/5-famous-bestsellers-that-were-rejected-and-50-more) Stephen King's *Carrie* was rejected 30 times. [Writing Routines](https://www.writingroutines.com/renowned-writers-on-overcoming-rejection/) Dr. Seuss was turned down by 27 publishers and was walking home to burn his manuscript when he ran into a friend who happened to be a children's book editor. [WildMind Writers](https://wildmindwriters.com/bookmarketing/6-famous-authors-who-once-faced-rejection)
